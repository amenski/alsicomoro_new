An "African restaurant near me" search returns three different kinds of results at once --- review directories, delivery marketplaces and the restaurants' own pages --- and they are not interchangeable. Knowing which kind of result you are looking at tells you how much to trust it, and what you still need to confirm before you travel across town or pick up the phone.

Why an "African restaurant near me" search gives different answers
------------------------------------------------------------------

Directories rank by rating and review volume. Delivery platforms rank by who will bring food to your address. Restaurant sites describe one kitchen only. A place can top a directory and still be closed on the night you want it; a place can appear on a delivery app and still be a twenty-minute drive outside your stated radius. Read the result type before you read the name.

Read the rating and the review count together
---------------------------------------------

A high score on very few reviews is weak evidence. On the same New York list, Tsion Cafe sits at 4.6, Meske at 4.2 from 134 reviews, and Kaia Wine Bar at 4.4 from 54 reviews ([Tripadvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurants-g60763-c1-New_York_City_New_York.html)). The middle score with the deepest review base is often the safer bet for a first visit, because it reflects a settled kitchen rather than a handful of enthusiastic early diners. Sort by review count before you sort by stars.

Confirm the exact branch before you set off
-------------------------------------------

Neighbourhood names are not addresses. Restaurants with more than one site --- [Le Baobab runs two locations, in Bed-Stuy and Harlem](https://ny.eater.com/maps/best-west-african-restaurants-nyc) --- appear once per branch on some platforms and once in total on others, and the reviews are not always kept apart. Check the street address on the listing against the address on the restaurant's own page, then check that the branch you want is the branch you booked.

Decide between delivery and dining in first
-------------------------------------------

Delivery availability is a property of the platform, not of the cuisine. Because marketplaces only list kitchens that have signed up, a healthy count of delivery options in a borough says nothing about the restaurants that have chosen to stay dining-room only. It also matters for food that is built around sharing: injera and the stews that go with it are served as a shared spread to be eaten by hand, and a thirty-minute journey in a box changes both the texture and the point of the meal. If what you actually want is the shared table, filter for places you can sit down in and ignore the delivery apps entirely.

Four questions to ask before you book
-------------------------------------

1. **Is the bread made in house?** For Eritrean and Ethiopian food this is the single biggest quality signal, and it is rarely stated on a directory listing.
2. **How is the spicing built?** Ask whether the berberè is made on site and what goes into it; a blend made from a dozen or more toasted and ground spices tastes different from a bought paste.
3. **Is the meal served to share?** If the dishes are meant for the centre of the table, the number of people in your party decides how many plates to order.
4. **Do you need a reservation?** Small rooms fill, and a restaurant with no delivery income depends on booked tables.

### Working through the checklist: an Eritrean and Ethiopian restaurant in Porta Venezia

Al Sicomoro answers all four before you ask. It prepares injera in house with three days of natural teff fermentation, makes its berberè from over twelve toasted and ground spices, and serves dishes at the centre of the table to be eaten by hand; the Ethiopian coffee ceremony is available on request. The menu is priced per dish --- Zighinì di carne €17, Kitfo €18, Zighinì vegetariano €16, Fir Fir vegetariano €15, Sambusa di carne €3, Sambusa di verdura €1,50, Categna €3 --- and it serves lunch and dinner Tuesday to Sunday. Because the dining room is small and there is no delivery, takeaway or online ordering, booking through TheFork, phone or WhatsApp is the practical route rather than turning up.

The same four questions work on any listing, in any city: they separate a restaurant that cooks its own food and expects you at a table from a name that simply appears near the top of the page.
