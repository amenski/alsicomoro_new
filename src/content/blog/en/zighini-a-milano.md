Zighinì is the stew most people order on their first Eritrean dinner. It is slow-cooked meat, or a spread of pulses and vegetables in the vegetarian version, built on a berberè base and served on top of injera, the sour fermented flatbread that doubles as plate and cutlery. In Milan most Eritrean and Ethiopian kitchens sit in and around Porta Venezia, so a search for zighini milano usually leads to the same few streets. This is what the dish is, how it is served, and how to order it without guessing.

What makes a good zighinì
-------------------------

### The berberè is the whole argument

Berberè is a toasted and ground spice blend, and its depth, not just its heat, is what separates a careful kitchen from a rushed one. A stew that tastes flat usually has a blend that was ground too far in advance or toasted too briefly. Al Sicomoro, in Porta Venezia, makes its berberè in house from over twelve toasted and ground spices.

### The injera underneath matters as much

Injera is made from teff flour and needs fermentation time to develop its sourness and its open, honeycombed surface. At Al Sicomoro the injera is prepared in house with three days of natural teff fermentation. Shortcut versions are thinner and drier, and tear instead of folding.

### It is a shared dish, eaten by hand

Zighinì arrives in the centre of the table, not plated per person. You tear a piece of injera, pinch up stew with it, and eat. The bread soaks up the sauce, and the meal moves at the pace of the table rather than the individual.

Meat and vegetarian zighinì
---------------------------

The two versions are different dishes rather than one recipe with a swap.

* **Zighinì di carne** is a beef or chicken stew in berberè. At Al Sicomoro it comes with shiro (chickpea purée), lentils and salad, and costs €17.
* **Zighinì vegetariano** is a spread of several preparations on one injera: red and yellow lentils, salad, shiro, hamli (chard), alicia (cabbage, beans and potatoes) and turnip with carrots. At Al Sicomoro it costs €16.

Every dish on the menu is served with homemade injera, so the bread is not a separate order. The full list, with the other stews, is on the [menu page](/en/menu.html).

How to order zighinì without over-ordering
------------------------------------------

Portions are generous and the bread is filling, so the common mistake is ordering a main each. The workable pattern for two people is two stews to share, one meat and one vegetarian, plus a couple of small plates to start.

### A worked example

Take Al Sicomoro's menu as the template. Order zighinì di carne (€17) and zighinì vegetariano (€16) between two people and you have both versions on the same table. Add fir fir vegetariano (€15), injera sautéed with tomato, onion and berberè, if you want a different texture rather than another stew. Kitfo (€18) is finely minced beef with clarified butter and mitmita, served cooked, raw or rare; it is a different dish, not a variant of zighinì. To start, sambusa di carne at €3, sambusa di verdura at €1,50 and categna at €3 are the cheap way to open the meal.

### Two things worth asking for

Ask how spicy the berberè is if you are sensitive to heat. And ask about the Ethiopian coffee ceremony when you book rather than at the table: it is offered on request and takes time, so it is best arranged in advance.

Where to eat it at Al Sicomoro
------------------------------

Al Sicomoro is at Via Panfilo Castaldi 19 in Porta Venezia. It serves lunch and dinner Tuesday to Sunday, 12:00–15:00 and 18:30–23:00, and is closed on Monday. The dining room is small, so book ahead by phone, on WhatsApp or through TheFork, especially for Friday and Saturday evenings; the details are on the [contact page](/en/contatti.html). There is no delivery, no takeaway and no online ordering, so zighinì here is a meal at the table.
