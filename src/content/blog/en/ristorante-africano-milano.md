Search for a ristorante africano Milano and you will be handed a single label that covers at least three unrelated kitchens. The addresses that come back range from West African cooking to Ethiopian and Eritrean tables to pan-African lounge bars, and the difference matters far more than the shared adjective. Knowing which tradition a place belongs to, and which day it is open, is most of the work of choosing well.

Three traditions, one search result
-----------------------------------

### West African cooking

This is the cuisine of Senegal, Nigeria and Ghana, and it is rare in the city. Ristorante Africano Mamma Chef is one of the few Milan addresses that puts West African dishes at the centre of its menu.

### Ethiopian and Eritrean cooking

The Horn of Africa tradition is the one most people mean when they talk about African food in Milan. It centres on injera, the fermented flatbread, and on berberè, the spiced chilli base, with dishes served on a shared platter and eaten by hand. Addresses in this group include Savana Ristorante Eritreo on Via Luigi Canonica 45, Ristorante Africa and Warsà in Porta Venezia, each with its own opening pattern.

### Pan-African and lounge formats

A third group mixes African cooking with a bar format. Kori Doro, on Via Felice Casati 22, describes itself as African gastronomy and a lounge bar, with lunch, aperitivo and dinner.

How to book without wasting a trip
----------------------------------

The practical failure mode with this category is arriving at a closed door. Work through four steps.

### 1. Fix the cuisine first

Decide whether you want West African cooking or the Ethiopian and Eritrean table. The two share nothing on the plate, and only a handful of addresses in Milan cover the first.

### 2. Check the day and the service window

Opening patterns vary widely. Some kitchens serve evenings only, others split lunch and dinner, and Sunday hours can differ from the rest of the week. Confirm the specific day rather than assuming a standard week.

### 3. Use the channel the restaurant actually answers

Phone is the standard route for several addresses in the city, some also take WhatsApp, and TheFork covers others. Few of these restaurants offer online ordering of any kind, so a booking is a booking, not an order.

### 4. Book ahead where the room is small

This is where Al Sicomoro is a useful worked example. The Porta Venezia restaurant, formerly known as Injera at the same address, takes reservations through TheFork, by phone and on WhatsApp, and recommends booking because the dining room is small. It serves lunch and dinner Tuesday to Sunday, 12:00–15:00 and 18:30–23:00, and is closed on Monday. There is no delivery, no takeaway and no online ordering, so the table is the only way to eat there. Phone, WhatsApp and directions are on the [contact page](/en/contatti.html).

What the food looks like once you sit down
------------------------------------------

At an Eritrean or Ethiopian table the centre of gravity is a shared platter. Dishes arrive together, the injera underneath soaks up the sauces, and the eating is done by hand. The detail worth asking about is the bread itself: at Al Sicomoro the injera is prepared in house with three days of natural teff fermentation, and the berberè is made with more than twelve toasted and ground spices.

Prices are per dish rather than per person, which makes a shared table easy to budget. Typical plates are Zighinì di carne at €17, Kitfo at €18, Zighinì vegetariano at €16 and Fir Fir vegetariano at €15, with Sambusa di carne at €3, Sambusa di verdura at €1,50 and Categna at €3. The Ethiopian coffee ceremony is available on request, and the room can be booked for private events.

Before you leave the house
--------------------------

Two practical points apply across the category. First, delivery and takeaway are largely absent, so a restaurant you cannot book is a restaurant you cannot eat from that evening. Second, several of the addresses above sit in and around Porta Venezia, so comparing two of them in one evening walk is feasible. For a table with house-made injera and a small room that fills, book Al Sicomoro ahead rather than turning up.
