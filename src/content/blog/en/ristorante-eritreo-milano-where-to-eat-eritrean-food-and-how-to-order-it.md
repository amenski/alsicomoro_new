What defines Eritrean and Ethiopian cooking
-------------------------------------------

Eritrean and Ethiopian restaurants in Milan share one backbone: injera, the spongy flatbread made from teff flour and left to ferment until it turns faintly sour. It works as plate, cutlery and bread at once. On top of it go stews seasoned with berberè, the toasted and ground chilli blend that gives the cuisine its heat and its depth.

Three habits follow from that. Food arrives to be shared, not portioned per person. It is eaten by hand, tearing off a piece of injera and using it to lift the stew. And the meal usually ends slowly, often with coffee roasted and brewed at the table. If you are looking for a quiet, individual-plate dinner, this is the wrong cuisine; if you want a table full of food and a long conversation, it is exactly right.

How a meal works: the worked example
------------------------------------

A typical order at an Eritrean restaurant follows a simple logic, and Al Sicomoro's menu shows it clearly. Start with a couple of sambusa --- small fried pastries, meat at €3 and vegetable at €1,50 --- plus categna, a thin flatbread at €3, to occupy the table while the main dishes arrive.

Then order two or three stews to share rather than one per person. A meat zighinì at €17 and a vegetarian zighinì at €16 side by side, or kitfo at €18 --- minced beef with spiced butter --- alongside fir fir vegetariano at €15, gives a table of two enough variety without over-ordering. Everything lands on a single large injera, and the injera itself is made in house with a natural teff fermentation lasting three days, which is what gives it the sour edge that balances the berberè.

The berberè here is ground in house from more than twelve toasted spices, so the heat is layered rather than flat. Ask for it on the side if you are unsure, and eat by hand --- the injera is the utensil.

What it costs
-------------

Prices are per dish, not per person, and dishes are designed to be combined:

* Zighinì di carne --- €17
* Kitfo --- €18
* Zighinì vegetariano --- €16
* Fir Fir vegetariano --- €15
* Sambusa di carne --- €3; Sambusa di verdura --- €1,50
* Categna --- €3

Because portions are shared, the sensible approach for two people is two mains plus one or two small plates, which keeps the bill in the mid-thirties --- comparable to a mid-range Milanese dinner, but with far more food on the table than an individual-plate menu would put there.

Hours, booking and what you cannot do
-------------------------------------

Service runs at lunch and dinner Tuesday to Sunday --- 12:00 to 15:00 and 18:30 to 23:00, with Monday closed --- and at Al Sicomoro reservations are taken through TheFork, by phone or on WhatsApp, specifically because the room fills. Current hours and contacts are on the [contact page](/en/contatti.html). Note also what is not offered --- no delivery, no takeaway and no online ordering. If you want this food, you have to sit down in the restaurant for it, which is part of the point: injera and stews do not survive a delivery box well.

The coffee ceremony and group bookings
--------------------------------------

The Ethiopian coffee ceremony is the natural close to the meal: green beans roasted, ground and brewed in front of the table, served in small cups, usually with incense. It takes time and is best requested when you book rather than at the end of the evening --- at Al Sicomoro it is available on request, and private events can be arranged for groups.

Quick checklist before you go
-----------------------------

* Decide on neighbourhood first: Porta Venezia is central and well connected.
* Book by phone, WhatsApp or TheFork --- walk-ins are risky at small rooms.
* Plan to share two or three dishes per two people, not one each.
* Ask for the berberè on the side if you are sensitive to heat.
* Request the coffee ceremony in advance, and skip any idea of takeaway.
