Searching for a ristorante eritreo Milano is not like looking for another pizza or risotto address. The cuisine has its own logic: fermented flatbread, spice-heavy stews, and a table built around sharing. The practical questions are what to order, what it costs, and how to secure a table in a small dining room.

What defines Eritrean and Ethiopian cooking
-------------------------------------------

Eritrean and Ethiopian cooking has a real presence in Milan. The backbone is injera, the spongy flatbread made from teff flour and left to ferment until faintly sour. It works as plate, cutlery and bread at once. On top go stews seasoned with berberè, the toasted and ground chilli blend that gives the food its heat and depth.

Three habits follow. Food arrives as shared dishes at the centre of the table, not portioned per person. It is eaten by hand, tearing off a piece of injera and using it to lift the stew. And the meal often ends slowly, with coffee roasted and brewed at the table. A quiet, individual-plate dinner is the wrong expectation; a table full of dishes and a long conversation is the right one.

How a meal works at Al Sicomoro
-------------------------------

At Al Sicomoro, the menu follows that logic clearly. The restaurant serves Eritrean and Ethiopian cuisine in Porta Venezia, and the ordering pattern is easy to copy.

### Small plates first

Start with a couple of sambusa — small fried pastries, meat at €3 and vegetable at €1,50 — plus categna, a thin flatbread at €3. These occupy the table while the main dishes arrive and give the meal a relaxed start.

### Mains to share

Then order two or three stews to share rather than one per person. A meat zighinì at €17 and a vegetarian zighinì at €16 side by side, or kitfo at €18 — minced beef with spiced butter — alongside fir fir vegetariano at €15, gives a table of two enough variety without over-ordering. Everything lands on a single large injera.

### Injera and berberè

The injera is made in house with a natural teff fermentation lasting three days, which is what gives it the sour edge that balances the berberè. The house berberè uses over twelve toasted and ground spices, so the heat is layered rather than flat. Ask for it on the side if you are unsure, and eat by hand: the injera is the utensil.

What it costs
-------------

Menu items are priced individually, not per person, and dishes are designed to be combined:

* Zighinì di carne — €17
* Kitfo — €18
* Zighinì vegetariano — €16
* Fir Fir vegetariano — €15
* Sambusa di carne — €3; Sambusa di verdura — €1,50
* Categna — €3

Because portions are shared, the sensible approach for two people is two mains plus one or two small plates. That gives more variety than an individual-plate menu would put on the table, without ordering more than the two of you can finish.

Hours, booking and practical limits
-----------------------------------

Al Sicomoro serves lunch and dinner Tuesday to Sunday, 12:00-15:00 and 18:30-23:00, with Monday closed. The dining room is at Via Panfilo Castaldi 19, 20124 Milano, in the heart of Porta Venezia. Reservations are taken via TheFork, phone and WhatsApp.

### Why booking matters

Booking is recommended because the dining room is small. Walk-ins can work at off-peak times, but a table booked in advance is the safer route for dinner, especially at the weekend.

### What is not available

There is no delivery, no takeaway and no online ordering. If you want this food, you have to sit down in the restaurant for it. That is part of the point: injera and stews do not survive a delivery box well.

The coffee ceremony and private events
--------------------------------------

At Al Sicomoro, the Ethiopian coffee ceremony is available on request. Green beans are roasted, ground and brewed in front of the table, served in small cups. It takes time and is best requested when you book rather than at the end of the evening. Private events can be arranged for groups.

Practical points before you go
------------------------------

* Two or three shared dishes are enough for two people; order by that logic rather than one main each.
* Reservations are advisable because the room is small.
* The coffee ceremony should be requested in advance.
* Delivery, takeaway and online ordering are not offered.

For a ristorante eritreo Milano, the practical move is to reserve ahead and arrive ready to share.
